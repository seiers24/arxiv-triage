## Examples

### Looking for papers related to a Workshop

Say we find a workshop, like the 3rd Workshop on Efficient and On-Device Generation.  We want to know:

1. What papers were submitted to this workshop?  Table-view. &#x20;
2. Out of the papers submitted to the workshop, which were clearly "the cream of the crop"?  Put more specifically, which papers posted findings that were most relevant to the workshop.&#x20;
3. Of the papers that were the "cream of the crop", what did these papers advertise?  Did they post empirical results?  Physical hardware measurements?  Etc...
4. Second to last, how are those papers directly relevant to what we want to do?  What findings were "revolutionary"?  Say the objective profile here specifically looks at how novel the papers are, their extendability, and how they can be built off of .&#x20;
5. Finally, how can we use these novel or "revolutionary" findings, and extend them?  How can we generate N proposals from those findings as well as other research?  Maybe the proposal doesnt generate here, maybe this information is simply stored in a database ready for retrieval when proposals need to be generated by user. &#x20;

## Searching for Relevant Research

Sometimes, you simply want to know if your research idea, or proposal, has been solved before.  You want to make sure your proposal is novel, and contributes in some way shape or form.  This requires the following:

1. A web search for all papers that are relevant to the research question your asking.  Basically searching key words, and seeing if they pop up.&#x20;
2. One agent sifting through all papers found that are relevant to the research question.  Not only looking at abstract, but also looking at the full paper, via html ideally, but with pdf fallback, and generating an output summary for each paper, combined into one typed dictionary as an output payload.
3. A separate agent must look at the output payload, and determine whether or not each of the summaries inside the payload directly solve our proposals problem/research question, or negatively impact the proposal in terms of novelty hits, or feasibility.  If uncertain, the agent will set a flag for the orchestrator.
4. Finally, a orchestrator must generate a summary based on the reading agent, and the output verification agent, on whether or not anything was found that directly impacts our proposal.  If the verification agent was uncertain about a specific paper, then that paper is escalated for human review.  Additionally, all records are kept of papers that were reviewed, including the summary generated and the output verification agents report.
